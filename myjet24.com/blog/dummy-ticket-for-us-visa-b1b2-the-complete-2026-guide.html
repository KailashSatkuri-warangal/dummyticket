How the US B1/B2 Visa Process Works (And Where Your Flight Itinerary Fits)

The U.S. nonimmigrant visa process is fundamentally different from Schengen, UAE, or most other systems. There is no VFS intermediary. There is no travel agency sponsorship. You apply directly to a U.S. Embassy or Consulate, and the centerpiece of the process is a face to face interview with a consular officer who has sole authority to approve or deny your application. The interview typically lasts three to five minutes, and decisions are often made within the first 60 seconds. If you are unsure which embassy or consulate handles your application, the embassy finder can help you locate the correct office for your nationality and location.

The application itself starts with the DS-160, the online nonimmigrant visa application form. This is where you enter your personal details, travel history, employment information, family connections, and your travel plans for the United States. The travel section of the DS-160 asks a specific question: "Have you made specific travel plans?" If you answer yes, the form requires your intended arrival date, departure date, and the locations you plan to visit. If you answer no, it asks for your estimated arrival date and intended length of stay.

Here is the critical point: the DS-160 asks for a travel itinerary. It does not ask for a confirmed, paid ticket. The State Department's own guidance lists a "travel itinerary, if you have already made travel arrangements" as one of the documents to have available when completing the form. The phrasing is conditional. You should have a travel itinerary because it strengthens your application and demonstrates concrete planning, but the system does not require a paid ticket. This is where a dummy ticket fits perfectly.

What Is Section 214(b) and How Does It Affect Your US Visa?

Every B1/B2 visa applicant walks into the interview carrying a legal burden that most people do not fully understand. Under Section 214(b) of the Immigration and Nationality Act, every nonimmigrant visa applicant is presumed to be an intending immigrant until they prove otherwise. The consular officer does not need to prove you plan to overstay. You need to prove you plan to return home. This is the single most common reason for visa refusal, and it drives the majority of the 2.4 million B1/B2 denials that occurred in FY2024.

Consular officers evaluate your intent to return through what they call "ties to your home country." These ties include employment (a stable job you would not abandon), property (real estate you own), family (a spouse and children waiting for you), financial obligations, and educational commitments. But your travel documentation also plays a direct role. A round trip flight reservation demonstrates that you have planned your departure from the United States. It signals that your trip has a defined beginning and end. A one way ticket, or no flight documentation at all, raises the obvious question: when exactly do you plan to leave?

This does not mean a flight reservation will overcome a 214(b) refusal on its own. No single document can do that. But a credible, verifiable round trip itinerary that aligns with your stated travel plans on the DS-160 removes one potential reason for the officer to say no. What Is the DS-160 Consistency Check and Why Does It Matter?

The DS-160 is not just a form. It is the foundation of your entire visa case. Every answer you provide becomes part of the record that the consular officer reviews before and during your interview. Inconsistencies between your DS-160 entries, your supporting documents, and your verbal answers are among the fastest ways to trigger a refusal.

Your flight itinerary must align with the DS-160 on four dimensions:

1. Dates. If your DS-160 says you plan to arrive on June 10 and stay for 14 days, your flight reservation should show arrival on or around June 10 and departure on or around June 24. A dummy ticket showing a three week stay when your DS-160 says two weeks creates a contradiction the officer will notice.

2. Destination. If your DS-160 lists a hotel in New York as your address in the United States, your flight should land at a New York area airport. An itinerary showing arrival in Los Angeles with a stated destination of New York raises a geographic logic gap.

3. Funding. The DS-160 asks who is paying for your trip. If you say you are self funded, your bank statements should reflect the means to cover the trip. If your flight reservation is for business class but your bank balance suggests you can barely afford economy, the mismatch becomes a credibility issue.

4. Purpose. A B1 business visa applicant whose flight itinerary shows a two month leisure trip creates confusion about the actual purpose of travel. Your itinerary should reflect your stated reason for visiting. Business travelers typically have shorter, more defined trips. Tourists have more flexibility but should still show a coherent plan.

The takeaway: your dummy ticket is not an isolated document. It is one piece of a narrative that must be internally consistent. Before submitting your DS-160, run a simple alignment check across your flight dates, your stated purpose, your funding evidence, and your intended address. If any element contradicts another, fix it before your interview. For a walkthrough on verifying that your reservation is real and retrievable, see the PNR verification guide.

What Changed in 2026: Higher Fees, Visa Bonds, and Tighter Screening

The U.S. visa landscape shifted significantly between late 2025 and early 2026. Understanding these changes is essential because they directly affect your financial exposure and how you should approach your flight documentation.

Application fees jumped to $435. On October 1, 2025, the State Department introduced a $250 Visa Integrity Fee on top of the existing $185 application fee. Both fees are non refundable regardless of the interview outcome. For a family of four applying together, that is $1,740 in fees before you have spent a dollar on airfare. To see how US visa costs compare to other destinations, the visa cost calculator breaks down fees across 80+ countries. This makes the case for using a dummy ticket instead of buying real tickets even stronger.

The Visa Bond Pilot Program expanded to 38 countries. Launched on August 20, 2025, and expanded in January 2026, the visa bond program requires nationals from designated countries to post a refundable bond of $5,000, $10,000, or $15,000 as a condition of visa issuance. Visas issued under the bond program are valid for a single entry within three months, with a maximum 30 day stay. Bond holders must enter and exit through one of nine designated airports (JFK, LAX, ORD, ATL, IAD, EWR, BOS, and the two major Canadian airports YYZ and YUL).

What Consular Officers Actually Look For in Your Flight Documentation

Consular officers do not have a checklist that says "flight ticket: yes or no." Their evaluation is holistic. They are assessing whether your overall profile, including your documents, your answers, and your demeanor, supports the conclusion that you will visit the United States temporarily and return home. Your flight documentation feeds into this assessment in several ways.

A round trip itinerary signals temporary intent. This is the most straightforward value of having a flight reservation. It shows the officer that you have planned both your arrival and your departure. A one way ticket, or no flight documentation at all, is not automatically disqualifying, but it removes a piece of evidence that supports your case.

Dates should match your stated purpose. A B1 applicant attending a five day conference should not show a flight reservation for a six week trip. A B2 tourist visiting family for two weeks should not have an open ended itinerary. The dates on your flight reservation should be proportional to and consistent with your stated reason for traveling.

The route should be realistic. Your dummy ticket should be on an airline that actually flies the route you are booking. A reservation showing a direct flight on a carrier that does not operate between your city and your U.S. destination will not survive even casual scrutiny. Use airlines that genuinely serve your route.

The PNR should be verifiable. While consular officers do not routinely verify PNR codes at the interview counter, they have the ability to do so. More importantly, if your case requires additional administrative processing (a 221(g) hold), your documents may be reviewed more thoroughly. A dummy ticket with a fabricated PNR is not just useless, it is grounds for a permanent visa ban under the misrepresentation provisions of INA Section 212(a)(6)(C). Never use an edited document. How Do You Get a Dummy Ticket for a US Visa Application?

Here is the process for handling your flight documentation for a US visa application, from DS-160 to interview day.

1. Determine your travel timeline. Start with your interview date, not your travel date. If your interview is in April and you plan to travel in June, your dummy ticket should reflect June travel dates. Do not create a reservation for dates that have already passed by the time of your interview.

2. Choose a realistic route. Select an airline that actually operates between your departure city and your U.S. destination. If you are flying from Mumbai to New York, book on carriers like Air India, United, or Emirates (connecting through Dubai). Check that the flight numbers and schedules are real.

3. Obtain a GDS reservation with a verifiable PNR. Use a dummy ticket service that creates real reservations through Amadeus, Sabre, or Travelport. The PNR must be retrievable on the airline's "Manage Booking" page or through tools like CheckMyTrip. Avoid free PDF generators that produce documents without real booking references. These will not survive verification.

4. Align with your DS-160. Before submitting your DS-160, ensure your dummy ticket dates, destination, and duration match what you enter on the form. If your dummy ticket shows 14 days in New York, your DS-160 should say 14 days with a New York area address. Run the four point alignment check: dates, destination, funding, purpose.

5. Print a hard copy for your interview. US visa interviews are conducted in person. Bring a printed copy of your flight reservation along with your other supporting documents. Have it organized in your document folder so you can present it quickly if the officer asks. Not all officers will ask for it, but having it ready demonstrates preparation.

6. Book your real ticket after approval. Once your visa is stamped, book your actual flights. Adjust dates as needed. The dummy ticket has served its purpose. If you are from a visa bond country and must use a designated port of entry, factor that into your real booking.

What Are the Special Flight Considerations for Visa Bond Countries?


Important: Designated Ports of Entry

If your country is on the visa bond list, your visa will be annotated with a bond requirement. You must enter and exit the United States through one of nine designated airports: Boston Logan (BOS), JFK, Washington Dulles (IAD), Newark (EWR), Atlanta (ATL), Chicago O'Hare (ORD), LAX, Toronto Pearson (YYZ), or Montreal (YUL). Your dummy ticket and your eventual real ticket must route through one of these airports. A flight booking to Miami, Houston, or San Francisco will not comply with bond conditions.


Bond visa holders also face a maximum 30 day stay and single entry restriction, regardless of the duration shown on their flight reservation. Your dummy ticket should reflect a stay of 30 days or less. Showing a 60 day itinerary when your bond visa limits you to 30 days creates an immediate inconsistency. The bond is refunded after you depart on time through a designated port, or after you properly file an extension of stay or change of status request.

What Should Indian Applicants Know?

India is the single largest source of B1/B2 visa applications globally. The FY2024 refusal rate of 16.32% is below the global average, but the sheer volume means hundreds of thousands of Indian nationals are refused every year. U.S. consulates in Mumbai, New Delhi, Chennai, Hyderabad, and Kolkata process massive application volumes, and interview wait times at some posts have stretched to 300+ days.

For Indian applicants, the flight itinerary carries particular weight because the 214(b) analysis focuses heavily on ties to India. A round trip reservation reinforces the narrative that you plan to return. One way documentation, or vague travel plans, feeds into the presumption of immigrant intent. Additionally, many Indian applicants are simultaneously in H-1B lotteries or have pending employment petitions, which triggers the enhanced scrutiny introduced by the September 2025 Proclamation. Your flight reservation should clearly support a temporary visit.

If you are an Indian national applying from the UAE (a very common scenario for expats), your ties analysis includes both your UAE residency and your connections to India. A dummy ticket showing a return to Dubai after your U.S. visit, with onward travel to India, can actually strengthen your case by demonstrating an established life outside the United States. What Should Filipino Applicants Know?

Filipino travelers face a unique double requirement. The U.S. consulate in Manila needs to see that you plan to return to the Philippines. But Philippine immigration also enforces outbound travel proof at the departure gate. If you cannot demonstrate to Philippine immigration officers that you have a return or onward ticket from your destination, they may deny you exit from the country.

This means Filipino applicants need a dummy ticket that works in two directions: it supports your U.S. visa application by showing round trip intent, and it satisfies Philippine departure requirements by confirming you have a return flight from the United States. The FY2024 B visa refusal rate for the Philippines stands at 28.33%, above the global average. Combined with the outbound enforcement, Filipino travelers have more at stake with their flight documentation than applicants from most other countries. What Should Nigerian Applicants Know?

Nigeria's FY2024 B visa refusal rate is 46.51%. Nearly half of all applications are refused. Added to the visa bond program in January 2026, Nigerian nationals now face an additional $5,000 to $15,000 financial commitment on top of the $435 application fee. A successful application from Nigeria can cost upward of $15,435 in fees and bonds alone, before airfare.

In this environment, buying a real ticket before visa approval is financially reckless. A Lagos to New York round trip typically costs $1,000 to $1,800. Combined with the bond and fees, a refused application could mean $17,000 in sunk costs. A dummy ticket reduces the airfare risk to a fraction of that amount. Nigerian applicants should also note the designated port of entry restriction: JFK, Newark, Atlanta, and Chicago are the most practical options for flights from Lagos or Abuja.

What Mistakes Get US Visa Applications Refused?

Based on patterns from consular refusals and immigration attorney analyses, these are the most common flight documentation errors in US visa applications:

1. Buying a non refundable ticket before the interview. The State Department tells you not to do this. If your visa is refused, you lose the ticket cost. Use a dummy ticket instead.

2. DS-160 date mismatch. Your flight dates do not match your DS-160 intended arrival and departure dates. Even a few days of discrepancy can raise questions.

3. One way ticket for a tourist visa. A one way ticket for a B2 tourist visa directly undermines your 214(b) case. It suggests you may not plan to return.

4. Destination city mismatch. Your DS-160 says New York, but your flight lands in Los Angeles. Officers notice geographic inconsistencies immediately.

5. Using a fabricated booking reference. An invented PNR is not just useless, it constitutes misrepresentation under INA 212(a)(6)(C)(i). This can result in a permanent visa ban. Never submit a document that cannot be verified.

6. Flight duration exceeding visa purpose. A B1 applicant attending a one week conference with a three month flight itinerary raises questions about actual intent.

7. Expired reservation. Your dummy ticket expired three days before your interview. The PNR now shows as cancelled. The officer sees a dead booking reference.

8. Ignoring the bond port of entry requirement. Bond country nationals who book flights to non designated airports face complications at entry, even with an approved visa.

How Is Flight Documentation Different for F-1 Student Visas?

While this guide focuses on B1/B2 visas, it is worth addressing the F-1 student visa briefly because many applicants and their families use dummy tickets for student visa interviews as well. The FY2024 F-1 refusal rate stands at 41%, significantly higher than B1/B2, making the financial case for avoiding premature ticket purchases even stronger.

The key difference for F-1 applicants is that a one way ticket is generally acceptable because students are traveling for an extended program. However, the flight itinerary should show travel to the city of the educational institution listed on the I-20 form. A student heading to the University of Michigan should not have a flight itinerary to Miami. The SEVIS ID, the I-20, and the flight destination must align. Student applicants who want to show return intent (which strengthens the 214(b) case) can use a dummy ticket showing a return flight after the academic term ends.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the US Embassy require a confirmed flight ticket for a B1/B2 visa?

No. The State Department's official guidance states that applicants should have a "travel itinerary, if you have already made travel arrangements." It explicitly advises not to purchase tickets until the visa is granted. A dummy ticket with a verifiable PNR satisfies the itinerary requirement without financial risk.

Will the consular officer verify my PNR during the interview?

Most officers do not run PNR checks during the three to five minute interview window. However, if your case goes to administrative processing (221(g)), your documents may be reviewed more thoroughly. The PNR should be real and verifiable at all times. A fabricated reference constitutes misrepresentation.

How long should my dummy ticket be valid for a US visa?

Your reservation should remain active through your interview date and ideally for a few weeks beyond, in case of administrative processing. US visa interview timelines vary widely by consulate. For posts with long wait times, ensure your dummy ticket dates reflect when you actually plan to travel, not when you are applying.

Can I answer 'No' to 'Have you made specific travel plans?' on the DS-160?

Yes. Answering no is acceptable if you have not finalized your plans. You will still need to provide an estimated arrival date and intended length of stay. However, having a flight reservation (even a temporary one) and answering yes demonstrates more concrete planning, which can strengthen your case.

What if my visa is approved but my dummy ticket expires before I travel?

That is the expected outcome. The dummy ticket serves its purpose during the application and interview phase. Once approved, you book your real flights based on your actual travel schedule.

I am from a visa bond country. Can I still use a dummy ticket?

Absolutely. The bond is a separate financial requirement assessed at the interview. Your flight documentation needs are the same. Just ensure your dummy ticket routes through a designated port of entry (JFK, LAX, ORD, ATL, IAD, EWR, BOS, YYZ, or YUL).

What happens if I get a 214(b) refusal? Is my dummy ticket wasted?

A 214(b) refusal is not permanent. You can reapply at any time with new or changed circumstances. Your dummy ticket cost is minimal compared to the $435 in non refundable application fees and any potential ticket losses. This is exactly why the dummy ticket approach exists.
