What Is a Dummy Ticket for Australia Visa?

A dummy ticket is a temporary flight reservation held in an airline’s booking system with a valid PNR (Passenger Name Record). It looks identical to a real airline itinerary — complete with passenger name, flight numbers, departure and arrival airports, dates, and a six-character booking reference — but it is not a paid, confirmed ticket.

For Australian visa applications, a dummy ticket serves as your proof of onward travel — evidence that you plan to leave Australia within your permitted stay. The Department of Home Affairs does not require a fully paid ticket; they require a credible travel plan showing your intended arrival and departure.

Who Qualifies for an ETA (Subclass 601)?

The Electronic Travel Authority is only available to passport holders from select countries: USA, Canada, UK, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Malaysia, Brunei, Hong Kong, and most Western European nations (Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, etc.). It costs just AUD 20 and is processed almost instantly via the official Australian ETA app.

If your country is not on the ETA list — including India, China, Indonesia, Philippines, Pakistan, Bangladesh, and most of Africa and South America — you must apply for the Subclass 600 Visitor Visa instead. This is where a dummy ticket becomes essential.

AI-Powered Visa Processing

Online applications are now triaged by an AI-enabled “rules engine” that checks biometrics against border-security databases. Clean, complete applications from low-risk applicants can be triaged and allocated to a case officer within hours. The government also hired 500 new case officers to support the reform.

Which Australia Visas Accept Dummy Tickets?

Here is a visa-by-visa breakdown of whether a dummy ticket works for your specific application:

Visitor Visa (Subclass 600) — Yes, Recommended

The Subclass 600 is the most common visa for tourists visiting Australia, and a dummy ticket is widely accepted and recommended. The Department of Home Affairs expects a travel itinerary showing your arrival and departure dates, but does not require a fully paid ticket. A verifiable flight reservation with a real PNR satisfies this requirement perfectly.

The Subclass 600 has four streams:

  • Tourist Stream: Tourism, holidays, visiting family/friends. Dummy ticket works.
  • Business Visitor Stream: Meetings, conferences. Dummy ticket works.
  • Sponsored Family Stream: Sponsored by Australian family member. Dummy ticket works.
  • Frequent Traveller Stream: For Chinese citizens. Dummy ticket works.

Student Visa (Subclass 500) — Yes, With Caveats

A dummy ticket can support your student visa application, but the flight itinerary is secondary to your Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE), financial evidence, and the Genuine Student (GS) test. Your dummy ticket should show a one-way arrival date that aligns with your course start date. A return flight is less critical for student visas since your stay matches your course duration.

2026 Update for Indian Students

India was reclassified to Evidence Level 3 (highest-risk category) from 8 January 2026. This means no streamlined processing, comprehensive documentation required upfront, and deeper verification. Your flight itinerary, financial proof, and Genuine Student responses must be impeccable. Use the Visa Risk Checker to assess your approval chances.

Working Holiday Visa (Subclass 417/462) — Yes

Working holiday applicants need to show they have sufficient funds (AUD 5,000+) and a return/onward flight plan. A dummy ticket provides the flight reservation component. For Indian applicants on the Subclass 462 ballot (only 1,000 places per year from 98,000+ applications), every detail of your application matters.

ETA (Subclass 601) & eVisitor (Subclass 651) — Less Critical

ETA and eVisitor applicants from eligible countries (US, UK, EU, Japan, etc.) typically face minimal documentation requirements. However, airlines may still check for a return or onward flight at check-in. Having a dummy ticket as backup can prevent boarding issues.

Transit Visa (Subclass 771) — Confirmed Ticket Required

Exception: For transit visas, Australia generally requires a confirmed onward ticket to your final destination. A dummy ticket may not be sufficient here. If you are transiting through Sydney, Melbourne, or another Australian airport, ensure you have a confirmed booking for the onward leg.

India to Australia — Country-Specific Guidance

India is the largest source of visa applicants for Australia, with over 120,000 student visas alone issued in 2024. If you are applying from India, here are the specific details you need:

Visa Options for Indian Citizens

  • Tourist Visa (Subclass 600): AUD 190, 1–3 weeks processing, 3/6/12 month stay
  • Student Visa (Subclass 500): AUD 2,000 — India reclassified to Evidence Level 3 from Jan 2026
  • Work and Holiday (Subclass 462): AUD 670, only 1,000 places/year via ballot (98,000+ applicants in 2024)
  • MATES Visa (Subclass 403): 3,000 places, STEM only, ages 18–30
  • Skilled Migration (189/190/491): Points-tested, requires skills assessment

India is NOT eligible for the ETA (601) or eVisitor (651). Indian passport holders must apply for the full Subclass 600 Visitor Visa, which requires comprehensive documentation including a flight itinerary.

Do Airlines at Indian Airports Check for Return Tickets?

Yes. Airlines face penalties for transporting passengers who are refused entry into Australia. Check-in agents at Delhi (DEL), Mumbai (BOM), Bengaluru (BLR), Hyderabad (HYD), and Chennai (MAA) routinely verify onward travel documentation. Having a dummy ticket with a verifiable PNR ensures a smooth check-in experience.

Transit Visa Pitfalls

Popular India-to-Australia routes often transit through Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Bangkok, or Dubai. Most of these do not require a separate transit visa if you stay airside. However, if your layover exceeds 24 hours or you want to leave the airport, you may need a transit visa for that country too. Check requirements for your transit country using our visa checker tool.

Financial Documentation Tips for Indian Applicants

  • Show 3–6 months of consistent bank transaction history — not a single large lump-sum deposit
  • Include salary slips, ITR (Income Tax Return), and Form 16 if employed
  • If sponsored, include the sponsor’s bank statements, employment proof, and a sponsorship letter
  • Property documents, fixed deposit certificates, and mutual fund statements strengthen your application
  • Demonstrate strong ties to India: employment, family, property ownership

Common Mistakes That Get Australia Visa Applications Rejected

Based on thousands of applications we have helped with, these are the most common reasons for Australia visa rejections:

1. Using Edited or Unverifiable Tickets

Submitting a Photoshopped or fabricated flight document is the fastest way to get rejected and potentially banned. Always use a service that provides real, verifiable dummy tickets with PNR codes that exist in airline systems.

2. Date Mismatches Across Documents

If your flight shows arrival on March 15 but your hotel booking starts March 17, that is a red flag. If your leave letter says “two weeks” but your flight shows a one-month stay, that is another red flag. Every date across every document must be consistent.

3. Insufficient Financial Evidence

A single large deposit days before applying looks suspicious. Show consistent income and a healthy balance over 3–6 months. For a 2-week tourist visit to Australia, having at least AUD 5,000–8,000 in accessible funds is advisable (no official minimum, but this demonstrates sufficiency).

4. Weak Ties to Home Country

Australia uses the Genuine Temporary Entrant (GTE) assessment for visitor visas. You must demonstrate strong reasons to return home: stable employment, family obligations, property ownership, or ongoing education. Applicants who cannot prove ties to their home country face higher rejection rates.

5. Submitting an Incomplete Application

With the new AI-powered processing system, incomplete applications can be fast-tracked to refusal within days. There is no grace period to add missing documents after submission. Submit a complete, “decision-ready” application from day one.

What Happens at the Airport and Australian Immigration

At Check-In

Airlines check for your visa grant notice and may ask about your return flight. If you used a dummy ticket for the visa application, you should have booked a real flight by this point (after visa approval). Carry both your visa grant letter and confirmed flight booking.

At Australian Border Force (Arrival)

Australian Border Force officers may ask:

  • “How long are you staying in Australia?”
  • “Do you have a return or onward flight booked?”
  • “Where are you staying?”
  • “How much money do you have for your trip?”

Have printed copies of your visa grant, flight booking, hotel reservation, and bank statement ready. While Australia increasingly uses SmartGates (automated passport control) for many nationalities, first-time visitors from countries like India typically go through manual immigration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a flight ticket before applying for an Australia visa?
No, you do not need a fully paid flight ticket. The Australian Department of Home Affairs requires a flight itinerary showing your intended travel dates, but a temporary flight reservation (dummy ticket) with a verifiable PNR is accepted. In fact, it is advisable not to buy an expensive ticket until your visa is approved.
Is a dummy ticket legal for Australia visa applications?
Yes, a dummy ticket is completely legal. It is a real, temporary reservation held in an airline’s booking system. The key is that the PNR must be verifiable — it must actually exist in the airline’s database. What is illegal is submitting a fabricated or Photoshopped document (an edited ticket). The Subclass 600 Visitor Visa costs AUD 190 for the Tourist Stream (applied from outside Australia). The Business Visitor Stream is also AUD 190. An optional Priority Consideration Service (available for Indian, Chinese, and UAE applicants) costs an additional ~AUD 1,000 and targets a 48-hour decision.
How long does the Australia visa take to process in 2026?
Following the March 2026 processing overhaul, the new targets are: Visitor Visa 1–3 weeks, Student Visa 2–4 weeks, Working Holiday under 1 day, and Skilled Worker Visa 3–5 weeks. Complete, “decision-ready” applications from low-risk applicants are processed fastest thanks to the new AI triage system.
Can Australian embassies verify my dummy ticket?
Yes, visa officers can check PNR codes against airline reservation systems. This is why using a verifiable dummy ticket from a reputable service like MyJet24 Premium ($7.90) is essential. What is the MATES visa and who is eligible?
The MATES (Mobility Arrangement for Talented Early-professionals Scheme) visa is a new Australian visa exclusively for Indian citizens aged 18–30 with a STEM degree obtained within the last 8 years. It offers 3,000 places per year through a digital ballot system, allows up to 2 years of living and working in Australia, and does not require employer sponsorship. Eligible STEM fields include renewable energy, mining, engineering, ICT, AI, FinTech, and AgriTech.
Do I need a return ticket to enter Australia?
While not always strictly required at immigration, having proof of onward or return travel is strongly recommended. Airlines often check for it at check-in (they face penalties for transporting passengers who are refused entry), and Australian Border Force officers may ask about your departure plans. For the visa application itself, a flight itinerary showing your intended return date is expected.

What happens if my visa is rejected after buying a flight ticket?
If you purchased a non-refundable ticket before your visa was approved, you could lose the entire amount — typically $800–$1,500 or more for flights to Australia. This is exactly why a dummy ticket is the safer approach: it gives the embassy what they need at zero financial risk to you. Only book your real flight after receiving your visa grant notification.
Is an onward ticket required for Australia from India?
For Indian citizens applying for an Australian tourist visa, proof of onward/return travel is a standard requirement in the application. Airlines at Indian airports (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai) also check for return flight documentation at check-in.
