Definition

What Is a Flight Itinerary (Flight Reservation) for a Visa?

A flight itinerary — sometimes called a plane itinerary — is the document that lists the flights of a trip: the passenger name, the route with departure and arrival airports, the dates and times, the airline and flight numbers, and usually a booking reference. It describes a travel plan. On its own it says nothing about whether the flights have been paid for.

Flight itinerary
A summary of the planned flights. Airlines, travel agents and booking sites issue one for a reservation, whether it has been paid or not.
Flight reservation (booking)
Seats held under a booking reference. A reservation can exist before payment and is released if it is not ticketed in time.
Flight ticket
A reservation that has been paid for and issued, identified by an e-ticket number. This is the document that lets you board.

For a visa application, embassies ask for the first two: a flight itinerary or reservation that shows when you enter and when you leave. A paid ticket is not required before the visa is approved, which is why the rest of this page is about what that itinerary must show.

Embassy Requirements

What Your Flight Itinerary Must Include for Visa Applications

Different embassies have slightly different requirements. Here is what consular officers look for across the most common visa applications in 2026:

Schengen Visa (29 countries)
Round-trip itinerary required. Must show entry into and exit from the Schengen Area within your visa validity. Flight dates should align with your intended travel dates on the application form. Booking reference must be visible.
UK Standard Visitor Visa
UKVI accepts flight itineraries and reservations. Your itinerary should show arrival in the UK and departure. Dates should be consistent with your accommodation bookings and bank statement.
US Visa (B1/B2 Tourist)
The US embassy does not officially require a confirmed flight booking before your interview. However, consular officers may ask about your travel plans. Having a flight itinerary demonstrates intent and preparedness.
Canada Visitor Visa (TRV)
IRCC (Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada) officially states that you should not buy a ticket before your visa is approved. A flight reservation or itinerary is sufficient for the application.
Australia Visitor Visa (subclass 600)
The Australian Department of Home Affairs accepts flight itineraries as part of a visa application. The document should show planned arrival and departure dates.
India e-Visa
The Indian e-Visa system requires applicants to enter arrival details. A flight reservation showing the arrival airport and date is sufficient — a confirmed ticket is not required at the application stage.
UAE / Dubai Visa
Most UAE visa sponsors accept flight reservations rather than confirmed tickets. Your itinerary should show arrival at Dubai International (DXB) and departure within your intended stay.
Thailand Tourist Visa
The Royal Thai Embassy requires a flight itinerary showing onward or return travel. Dates should not exceed 30 days (for the standard visa exemption). A round-trip itinerary is strongly recommended.

Visa Centers

Submitting Through VFS Global, TLScontact or BLS: What Actually Gets Checked

Most Schengen and UK applications never touch the embassy directly — they go through outsourced visa centers (VFS Global, TLScontact, BLS International). Knowing what the counter staff actually do with your flight itinerary removes most of the anxiety around the appointment:

Completeness, not authenticity
The counter clerk checks your file against the embassy checklist: is a flight itinerary present, does the name match the passport, do the dates match the application form. Visa centers do not verify bookings with airlines — the assessment happens later, at the consulate.
Bring it printed on A4
Centers scan paper files. A PDF on your phone screen is not accepted at most VFS and TLS counters. Print the itinerary in portrait, uncropped, so the booking reference and dates stay readable after scanning.
Dates must agree across documents
The most common reason files bounce at the counter: flight dates that contradict the application form, the travel insurance certificate, or the hotel booking. Fix any mismatch before your appointment — a resubmission means booking a new appointment slot.
A reservation is the expected document
Center checklists ask for a "flight reservation / itinerary" — staff process unpaid reservations all day, because that is the standard document at application stage, consistent with official guidance not to buy tickets before approval.

After Approval

After Your Visa Is Approved: Booking the Real Flight

Once your passport comes back with the visa stamp, you have a window to convert your dummy itinerary into actual travel. The mistake most travelers make at this stage is rushing to book the first available flight at any price. The smart approach is a 5–7 day window of comparison, fare-class awareness, and risk hedging.

Compare fares across 3+ aggregators
Check Google Flights, Skyscanner and Kayak for the same route. Prices vary by 10–30% across platforms even for identical flights. Use the dates from your dummy itinerary as your starting point.
Choose a refundable or changeable fare for the outbound
Pay 10–20% more for a fare class that allows changes. If the airline cancels, weather disrupts, or you need to delay, you avoid the loss. The economy basic fare often saves $50 but costs $200+ in change fees if anything goes wrong.
Book seats matching your dummy itinerary dates first
If your visa specifies particular travel dates or has narrow validity, prioritize those dates. Cheap fares 2 days outside your dummy itinerary are not a saving if your visa is invalid for them.
Confirm baggage and visa-stamp transfer rules
Some carriers require visa verification at check-in. Have a clear digital scan of your visa page ready. For e-visas, print the approval email — gate staff sometimes can't verify on their own systems.
Arrange travel insurance with cancel-for-any-reason cover
Once you have a real ticket, insurance prices drop because the booking date is closer to travel. Cancel-for-any-reason cover (CFAR) costs 30–40% more but reimburses up to 75% if anything makes you cancel.
Processing Delays

What Happens If Your Visa Takes Longer Than Expected?

Embassies miss their published processing times more often than they admit — especially in peak season (April–August for Schengen, December for India). If your travel date is approaching and the visa hasn't arrived, here's the realistic playbook:

10+ days before travel
You still have options
Contact the consulate to request status. Some embassies (Italy, Spain) allow expedited processing for a fee at this stage. Do not buy real tickets yet.
5–10 days before travel
Make backup plans
Tentatively reserve refundable flights as Plan B. Re-check daily. If the visa arrives, you can confirm; if not, cancel without loss.
Less than 5 days before travel
Reschedule trip
Push your travel date by 1–2 weeks. The dummy itinerary on your application no longer matters at this point — what matters is when you actually fly. Some applicants buy a refundable ticket as final hedge.
Visa arrives after intended travel date
Use the validity window
Schengen visas are typically valid for 90 days within a 180-day rolling period. UK visas are issued for 6 months. You don't lose the visa — you simply travel later within validity.
FAQ

Flight Itinerary for Visa — 18 Questions Answered

What is a flight itinerary for a visa application?
A flight itinerary for a visa application is a document showing your planned travel route — the departure airport, destination, dates, passenger name, and a booking reference. It is required by most embassies and consulates as part of the visa application checklist to demonstrate that you have planned your trip and intend to travel during the visa validity period.
Does the embassy or consulate verify if my flight itinerary is real?
Embassies do not systematically call airlines to verify individual bookings. They accept flight itineraries and reservations as part of the application package. The document serves to demonstrate your planned travel — consular officers are focused on your overall application, not on confirming each booking individually.
What must a flight itinerary contain for a Schengen visa?
For a Schengen visa, your flight itinerary must show: your full name as it appears on your passport, a round-trip itinerary showing entry into and exit from the Schengen Area, your planned travel dates (consistent with other application documents), a booking reference, and the flight numbers and airlines. A QR code or barcode adds credibility but is not always required.
Does the US embassy (B1/B2) require a flight itinerary?
The US embassy does not list a flight itinerary as a mandatory document for B1/B2 tourist or business visa applications. However, consular officers interview every applicant and may ask about travel plans. Having a flight itinerary ready demonstrates preparation and clear travel intent, which can strengthen your application.
What flight details does the UK Visa and Immigration office require?
UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) guidance states that you should provide evidence of your travel plans. A flight itinerary showing your arrival in the UK and planned departure is accepted. The dates should be consistent with your accommodation bookings, bank statements, and the duration you are applying for.
Can I submit a one-way flight itinerary for a visa application?
It depends on the embassy. Most Schengen embassies require a round-trip itinerary showing you will leave the Schengen Area. One-way itineraries are sometimes accepted when combined with evidence of onward travel to a third country (e.g., a connecting flight to another destination). For UK, US, and Canadian visas, a one-way ticket with a strong explanation of your travel plan can be accepted.
What is the difference between a flight itinerary and a booking confirmation?
A flight itinerary shows your planned route, dates, and passenger details — it may or may not be a paid booking. A booking confirmation is a paid, confirmed reservation from an airline with a full e-ticket number. For visa applications, a flight itinerary (which may be a reservation or a dummy ticket with a booking reference) is what embassies ask for — a full paid booking confirmation is not required.
Does the Australian High Commission accept a flight reservation for a visa?
Yes. The Australian Department of Home Affairs (DHA) accepts flight itineraries and reservations as part of a visitor visa (subclass 600) application. The document should show your planned arrival in Australia and departure, with dates that fit within your intended stay.
What information must appear on a flight itinerary for visa purposes?
A valid flight itinerary for visa purposes must include: full passenger name (matching passport), departure airport and destination airport with IATA codes, flight number and airline name, departure and arrival dates and times, a booking reference, and the itinerary type (one-way or round-trip). A QR code strengthens the document further.
Is a flight reservation the same as a flight itinerary for a visa?
In visa application terminology, these terms are used interchangeably. A flight reservation, flight itinerary, dummy ticket, and temporary booking all refer to the same type of document — a planned travel route with a booking reference that satisfies the embassy flight requirement. None of these require a paid, confirmed airline ticket.
Does the Canada visa office accept flight itineraries for visitor visas?
Yes. IRCC official guidance states that applicants should not purchase tickets before visa approval. A flight reservation or itinerary showing your planned travel to and from Canada is the correct document to include in your visitor visa (TRV) application.
Should the hotel accommodation dates match the flight itinerary dates?
Yes. Embassies cross-reference your documents. Your flight arrival date should match your first accommodation night, and your departure date should match your last accommodation checkout. Inconsistencies between your flight itinerary, hotel bookings, and visa application dates are a red flag for consular officers and can delay or jeopardise your application.
Do I need to include connecting flights in my visa itinerary?
Yes, if you have connecting flights. Your itinerary should show the complete journey — including any stopovers or connections — not just the final destination. If you are transiting through a third country to reach your destination, include those flights in the itinerary.
How many weeks before travel should my flight itinerary be dated?
Your itinerary dates should reflect your actual planned travel dates, not the current date. Embassies want to see when you plan to travel — typically within the validity of the visa you are applying for. For Schengen visas, allow 2–6 weeks for processing. Your itinerary should show departure dates that fall within this processing window.
Does an Indian e-Visa application require a confirmed flight booking?
The Indian e-Visa online form asks for your port of arrival (airport), flight number, and expected date of arrival. A flight itinerary or reservation with this information is sufficient. A confirmed, paid ticket is not required. Your e-Visa is typically approved within 72 hours, after which you can book real flights.

Is there a flight itinerary template for a visa application?
Embassies do not publish an official template, but they all expect the same fields: passenger name as in the passport, airports with IATA codes, flight numbers, dates and times, a return or onward flight and a booking reference.
