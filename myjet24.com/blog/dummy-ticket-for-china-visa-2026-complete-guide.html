Last February, a software developer from Bangalore named Priya arrived at Beijing Capital International Airport with a one-way ticket, a laptop bag, and the kind of confidence that comes from having traveled to 23 countries without a single visa issue.

China was number 24. And China didn’t care about the other 23.

The immigration officer asked three questions: Where is your return flight? Where are you staying? How long are you planning to stay?

Priya had booked a hotel. She had bank statements showing plenty of funds. But she had no return or onward ticket — because she planned to book one once she knew her exact departure date. She was denied entry and put on the next flight back to Delhi.

What Priya didn’t know is that China’s entry requirements in 2026 operate on a completely different logic than most countries. The rules depend not just on where you’re going, but on which passport you hold, which entry route you’re using, and what type of documentation your specific consulate demands.

Let’s make sure you don’t make the same mistake.

Quick answer

China’s entry landscape has changed dramatically in 2026. Citizens of 50 countries can enter visa-free for 30 days, 55 countries qualify for 240-hour visa-free transit, and the new online COVA system has reshaped how everyone else applies. Whether you need a dummy ticket depends entirely on your passport and entry route — and getting it wrong can mean a 40% rejection rate, as Indian applicants are currently discovering.

China’s Three Entry Pathways in 2026

Before we talk about dummy tickets, you need to understand the three fundamentally different ways people enter China in 2026. Each has different documentation requirements.

Pathway 1: 30-Day Visa-Free Entry (50 Countries)

As of February 2026, citizens of 50 countries can enter China without a visa for up to 30 days. This is a massive expansion from pre-pandemic policies.

Eligible countries include:

EuropeAustria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Malta, Monaco, Montenegro, Netherlands, North Macedonia, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, UK, Russia, Andorra
Asia-PacificJapan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Brunei, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain

What you need at the border:

  • Valid passport (minimum 6 months validity)
  • Proof of accommodation (hotel booking or invitation)
  • Proof of onward or return travel

That last point is critical. Even though you don’t need a visa, immigration officers routinely ask for proof that you’re leaving within 30 days. A free dummy ticket from MyJet24 satisfies this requirement instantly — no booking fees, no financial risk, and you get a professional PDF with a valid booking reference in 30 seconds.

Pro tip

If you’re entering visa-free, your dummy ticket should show a departure date within 30 days of your arrival. Officers specifically check this timeframe.

Pathway 2: 240-Hour Visa-Free Transit (55 Countries)

This is China’s most generous transit program, expanded in 2026 to 65 entry ports across 24 provinces.

The rules:

  • You must be transiting through China to a third country
  • Your stay cannot exceed 240 hours (10 days)
  • You need a confirmed onward ticket to a country different from where you came from
  • You can now travel freely within the 24 participating provinces

The 55 eligible countries include all 50 visa-free countries listed above, plus Mexico, Singapore, Ukraine, Serbia, and Qatar.

Why a dummy ticket is essential for transit: The 240-hour transit policy has one non-negotiable requirement: you must have a confirmed ticket to a third destination. Airlines will deny you boarding without it, and immigration will deny entry.

Here’s the scenario: you’re flying from London to Bangkok and want to spend a week exploring Shanghai and Beijing on the way. You need a confirmed London → Shanghai ticket and a confirmed Shanghai → Bangkok ticket. If your Bangkok leg isn’t booked yet, a dummy ticket showing your onward journey is exactly what you need.

Major eligible entry ports:

  • Beijing Capital (PEK) & Beijing Daxing (PKX)
  • Shanghai Pudong (PVG) & Shanghai Hongqiao (SHA)
  • Guangzhou Baiyun (CAN)
  • Chengdu Tianfu (TFU)
  • Shenzhen Bao’an (SZX)
  • Hangzhou, Kunming, Xiamen, and 55+ additional ports

Pathway 3: Standard Visa Application (Everyone Else)

If your country isn’t on either list above — or if you’re staying longer than 30 days — you need a Chinese visa. This is where it gets complicated, because requirements vary dramatically by nationality.

Who Actually Needs a Dummy Ticket for a China Visa in 2026?

This is the most important section of this guide, because the answer isn’t the same for everyone.

US Citizens: Simplified Requirements

As of January 1, 2026, the Chinese Embassy in the US no longer requires flight itineraries, hotel bookings, or invitation letters for standard tourist (L) visa applications. You still need a valid passport, completed COVA application form, recent passport photo, and previous Chinese visas if applicable.

Bottom line: If you’re American and applying for a tourist visa, you technically don’t need a dummy ticket for the application. However, you’ll still want proof of onward travel for the actual entry at immigration.

Indian Citizens: Critical — You Need Everything

This is where the story gets serious. Chinese consulates in New Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai have introduced a new online pre-approval process in late 2025 that has fundamentally changed the game.

The current situation:

  • Rejection rates have climbed to nearly 40%
  • The new COVA online system requires uploading all documents before you can book a biometrics appointment
  • Confirmed round-trip flight tickets are explicitly required
  • Bank statements showing 6 months of transaction history are mandatory
  • Processing times have doubled to 3–4 weeks

Why a dummy ticket is essential for Indian applicants: Buying a real flight ticket before your visa is approved is a massive financial risk — especially when 4 out of 10 applications are being rejected. A round-trip flight to China from India can cost £30,000 to £80,000 or more.

A dummy ticket from MyJet24 Premium ($7.90) gives you a verifiable flight reservation with a real PNR code that Chinese consulates can check. If your visa is approved, you book your real flight. If it’s rejected, you’ve lost nothing.

Important

Make sure your dummy ticket dates match your hotel booking dates and your intended travel period exactly. The COVA system flags inconsistencies automatically.

Other Nationalities

For most other nationalities that require a visa, the standard L (tourist) visa application requires:

  • Completed COVA application form
  • Valid passport with 6+ months validity and 2 blank pages
  • Passport photo (48mm × 33mm, white background)
  • Round-trip flight itinerary or reservation
  • Hotel booking confirmation
  • Travel itinerary showing day-by-day plans

The flight itinerary requirement varies by consulate. But having a professional flight itinerary for your visa application eliminates any guesswork and ensures your application is complete.

The 10 Biggest China Visa Mistakes in 2026

Based on current rejection data and consulate feedback, these are the errors that get applications denied:

1. Mismatched Dates

Your flight dates, hotel dates, travel insurance dates, and itinerary dates must align perfectly. A single day’s gap triggers automatic review.

2. Buying Real Tickets Before Approval

With a 40% rejection rate for Indian applicants and unpredictable processing for other nationalities, buying non-refundable tickets before your visa is approved is a gamble. Use a free dummy ticket instead.

3. Using Edited PDFs Instead of Real Reservations

Chinese consulates verify PNR codes. A Photoshopped ticket or a PDF from a random generator will get your application rejected — and potentially flagged for fraud. Read our guide on how to verify your dummy ticket PNR.

4. Insufficient Financial Proof

Bank statements must show consistent income over 6 months. Sudden large deposits look suspicious. For detailed requirements, check our bank statement for visa application guide.

5. Incomplete COVA Forms

The new online system rejects incomplete forms automatically. Double-check every field before submission.

6. Wrong Photo Specifications

China requires 48mm × 33mm photos with a white background. Many applicants submit photos that meet other countries’ standards but not China’s.

7. Passport Validity Issues

Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from your intended entry date, with at least 2 completely blank pages.

8. No Cover Letter

While not always mandatory, a well-written cover letter explaining your travel purpose significantly improves approval chances.

9. Applying Too Late

Standard processing takes 7–10 working days, but can extend to 3–4 weeks during peak season. Apply at least one month before your planned departure.

10. Ignoring the Transit Option

Many travelers who need a short China stopover apply for a full tourist visa when they could use the 240-hour visa-free transit. Before applying, check if you qualify — it’s faster, free, and only requires a valid passport and a confirmed onward ticket.

240-Hour Visa-Free Transit: A Step-by-Step Walkthrough

Since this is one of the most popular — and most misunderstood — ways to visit China in 2026, let’s walk through it completely.

Scenario: You’re a UK citizen flying from London to Sydney and want to spend 7 days exploring Beijing and Shanghai.

What you need:

  1. Your UK passport (valid 6+ months)
  2. Your London → Beijing flight ticket (real or reserved)
  3. A confirmed Beijing/Shanghai → Sydney ticket (or a dummy ticket showing your onward journey)
  4. A hotel booking for your China stay

At the airport (London): The airline checks your UK passport, confirms you’re from one of the 55 eligible countries, verifies your onward ticket to Sydney via TIMATIC, and issues your boarding pass. No visa needed.

At immigration (Beijing): You proceed to the “Visa-Free Transit” lane. The officer checks your passport and nationality, your onward ticket to a third country (Sydney), that your stay is under 240 hours, and that you entered through an eligible port. You receive a temporary entry stamp valid for 240 hours.

Important

Your onward destination must be a different country than your origin. London → Beijing → London does not qualify. London → Beijing → Sydney or London → Beijing → Tokyo does qualify.

Airlines Flying to China in 2026

China’s “Big Three” carriers have massively expanded their international networks in 2026:

  • Air China — Flag carrier, headquartered in Beijing. New daily routes from Beijing Daxing to Frankfurt and Milan Malpensa.
  • China Eastern — Shanghai-based, operating 20 China-Europe routes in summer 2026 with ~1.9 million two-way seats.
  • China Southern — Guangzhou-based, launched Beijing Daxing-Helsinki service. Largest carrier in Asia by fleet size.

All airlines use the TIMATIC system to verify entry requirements before boarding. If your destination requires proof of onward travel, the gate agent will check before you board. Having your dummy ticket PDF ready saves time and prevents boarding delays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a dummy ticket if my country has visa-free access to China?

Yes, in most cases. Visa-free doesn’t mean document-free. Immigration officers routinely ask for proof of a return or onward flight within your allowed stay period. A free dummy ticket takes 30 seconds to generate and prevents potential issues at the border.

Can Chinese consulates verify my dummy ticket’s PNR code?

Yes. A legitimate dummy ticket from a service like MyJet24 Premium ($7.90) contains a real booking reference that exists in airline reservation systems. Consulate staff can and do verify these. This is exactly why you should never use an edited PDF — read our guide on dummy ticket scams to understand the difference.

How long should my dummy ticket be valid?

For visa applications, your ticket dates should cover your intended travel period. For 30-day visa-free entry, the return date should be within 30 days. For 240-hour transit, within 10 days of arrival. Generate your ticket close to your application or travel date for maximum relevance.

Is a dummy ticket legal for Chinese visa applications?

Absolutely. A dummy ticket is a real, temporary flight reservation — not a fabricated document. Chinese consulates, like consulates worldwide, accept flight reservations as part of visa applications. The legality of dummy tickets is well-established in international travel.

What’s the difference between a dummy ticket and an edited ticket?

A dummy ticket contains a real PNR that can be verified in airline systems. An edited ticket is a PDF with no real booking behind it. Using an edited ticket is document fraud and can result in visa bans. Our article on what a dummy ticket actually is explains this distinction in detail.

I’m Indian and my China visa was rejected. Can I reapply?

Yes, but you need to address the rejection reason first. Common issues include insufficient financial proof, missing flight documentation, and incomplete COVA forms. Read our visa refused recovery guide for a step-by-step reapplication framework.

Do I need a hotel booking as well as a flight reservation?

For visa applications, yes — most consulates require both. For visa-free entry, it’s strongly recommended. MyJet24 also offers hotel booking confirmations for visa applications that pair perfectly with your dummy ticket.

Which Chinese airports support 240-hour visa-free transit?

As of 2026, 65 ports across 24 provinces participate. All major airports — Beijing (PEK, PKX), Shanghai (PVG, SHA), Guangzhou (CAN), Chengdu (TFU), Shenzhen (SZX), and many more — are included.

Can I use the 240-hour transit to enter and leave from the same country?

No. This is the most common misunderstanding. You must be traveling to a third country. If you fly from Tokyo to Beijing, you must continue to a country other than Japan. The transit must involve at least three countries.

How much does a China tourist visa cost?

Fees vary by nationality. US citizens pay approximately $185 plus the new $250 Visa Integrity Fee ($435 total). Indian citizens pay approximately ₹3,900 for a single-entry visa. European citizens from visa-free countries pay nothing. Check our visa cost calculator for your specific nationality.
