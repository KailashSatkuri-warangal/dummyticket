Indonesia Passport: Where Can You Travel in 2026?

The Indonesian passport ranks 70th–73rd globally in the 2026 Henley Passport Index, granting visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to roughly 75 destinations — the strongest passport in Southeast Asia after Singapore, Brunei, and Malaysia. With 279 million Indonesian citizens and an estimated 4.7 million Indonesian migrant workers (PMI — Pekerja Migran Indonesia) abroad, alongside the largest annual Hajj quota of any country worldwide, Indonesia is among the world's largest sources of outbound visa applications.

The Indonesian visa-application landscape is shaped by four distinct flows. ASEAN visa-free travel to all nine fellow ASEAN states under the ASEAN Framework Agreement on Visa Exemption gives Indonesians a strong regional baseline. Hajj and Umrah pilgrimage is the largest single religious-travel programme on Earth — Saudi Arabia allocates Indonesia's annual Hajj quota at over 221,000 pilgrims (the largest national quota globally), with Umrah travel adding millions more annually. Migrant-worker placement through BP2MI (Indonesian Migrant Workers Protection Agency) channels labour to Saudi Arabia, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Malaysia, Singapore, and South Korea under sector-specific bilateral arrangements. Tourist and family-visit travel to Australia, the UK, the US, and Schengen Europe rounds out the picture, supported by Indonesia's growing middle class.

Two structural shifts have reshaped the landscape since 2023. First, the Indonesian e-passport rollout — fully operational across Indonesia's main immigration offices — has materially improved acceptance at premium destinations and unlocked the Japan visa-free arrangement specifically for e-passport holders. Second, Saudi Arabia's tourist eVisa expansion (2024) opened a non-Hajj/non-Umrah Saudi entry path that did not previously exist for Indonesian travellers. This guide details which destinations require advance visas, where Indonesia maintains diplomatic representation, and the specific supporting documents — Indonesian KTP/NPWP cross-references, BP2MI clearance for migrant workers, and bank-issued forex receipts — that consular officers expect from Indonesian applicants in 2026.

Top Destinations for Travelers

The top destinations for Indonesian passport holders in 2026 reflect Indonesia's ASEAN integration, the world's largest pilgrim flow to Saudi Arabia, established migrant-worker corridors to East Asia and the Gulf, and a fast-growing leisure-travel market:

  • ASEAN states (Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Brunei) — All visa-free for Indonesian passport holders for tourist stays of 14–30 days under the ASEAN Framework Agreement. Singapore and Malaysia are the most-travelled — both for tourism and shopping. Thailand and Vietnam have grown rapidly as Indonesian honeymoon and group-tour destinations. ASEAN visa-free entry typically requires a confirmed return ticket and accommodation evidence at port of entry.
  • Saudi Arabia — Indonesia's largest single visa-issuing market and the world's largest Hajj programme. Hajj quotas (over 221,000 pilgrims annually, expanded post-2023) are allocated through the Indonesian Ministry of Religious Affairs and accredited Hajj group organisers (KBIH/PIHK); Umrah visas via the Saudi Tasreeh portal through licensed Indonesian Umrah agents. The Saudi tourist eVisa is now also available to Indonesians directly via the Visit Saudi portal — a 2024 expansion that bypasses traditional embassy and Hajj/Umrah channels for tourism only.
  • Japan — Visa-free for Indonesian e-passport holders under the bilateral agreement (introduced 2014, ongoing). Indonesians travelling on machine-readable passports must apply for a tourist visa at the Embassy of Japan Jakarta or consulates in Surabaya, Medan, Denpasar, or Makassar. Japan's Visit Japan Web pre-arrival declaration system streamlines arrival processing further.
  • South Korea — Visa-required for Indonesian passport holders. Tourist visa applications are processed at the Embassy of the Republic of Korea Jakarta and the K-VAC visa application centre. K-ETA is required for those exempted from a visa under specific categories. Standard processing 5–10 working days. South Korea has become a major Indonesian migrant-worker destination under the EPS (Employment Permit System) bilateral arrangement.
  • Hong Kong / Macao — Both visa-free for Indonesian passport holders for short tourist stays. Hong Kong's 30-day visa-free entry for Indonesians has driven significant labour migration alongside tourist flows.
  • Taiwan — Visa-free under specific conditions (must hold a valid US, UK, Canadian, Japanese, Korean, Schengen, Australian, or NZ visa or permanent residence) — otherwise visa-required. The Travel Authorisation Certificate (TAC) e-system streamlines applications for eligible Indonesians.
  • United Arab Emirates — Visa-on-arrival, free, valid for 30 days for Indonesian passport holders. A popular Indonesian holiday and shopping destination, alongside being a transit hub for Hajj/Umrah travel.
  • Türkiye — e-Visa required for Indonesian passport holders; apply online at evisa.gov.tr; processing typically minutes. Single-entry 30-day tourist eVisa.
  • United Kingdom — Standard Visitor Visa required. Application is fully online via gov.uk; biometrics submitted at VFS Indonesia (Jakarta and Surabaya). Standard processing is 3 weeks but actual times in 2025–2026 trend longer (4–6 weeks). Indonesian student-visa applications (Tier 4) have grown rapidly with UK university recruitment in Jakarta and Bandung.
  • Australia — Visitor visa (subclass 600) applied online via ImmiAccount. Processing time has lengthened to 2–4 months in 2025–2026 — apply early. Indonesian student-visa (subclass 500) and Working Holiday Maker (subclass 462) volumes are also significant.
  • United States — B1/B2 non-immigrant visa required. Applications processed at the US Embassy Jakarta and Consulate Surabaya. Indonesian B1/B2 wait times have eased significantly since 2023 — first-time interview slots typically range 2–6 months in current cycles, dropbox renewals within 1–3 weeks if eligible.
  • Schengen Area (Netherlands, Germany, France, Italy) — Visa-required across all 29 Schengen states. The Netherlands historically has the deepest bilateral visa relationship with Indonesia (driven by post-colonial ties and the large Dutch Indo community); Germany, France, and Italy handle significant volumes. VFS Global Jakarta and TLScontact handle most applications.
  • Maldives + Bali transit alternatives — Maldives offers free 30-day visa-on-arrival; popular Indonesian honeymoon destination.

Common Visa Rejection Reasons

Indonesian passport applicants encounter mid-tier rejection rates — better than other South Asian source countries but with specific pattern weaknesses at Schengen, US, and Australian missions. The five most common rejection reasons for Indonesian applicants are:

  1. Insufficient evidence of return ties to Indonesia. The leading refusal cause across Schengen, UK, US, and Australian visa applications. Strongest evidence: long-term employment with NPWP-registered (Tax ID) employer and verifiable BPJS Kesehatan and Ketenagakerjaan deductions, property ownership (Sertifikat Hak Milik / SHGB), business registration with NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha) under OSS, dependents in Indonesia (KTP/Kartu Keluarga cross-references), and recent fixed-deposit (Deposito) certificates from a Bank Indonesia-licensed bank.
  2. Bank statement issues — sudden deposits. Schengen, UK, US, and Australian visa officers look closely at the 6-month transaction pattern in Indonesian bank statements. Sudden round-figure deposits (IDR 50,000,000 / 100,000,000) appearing 3–4 weeks before application are flagged as third-party funding. Maintain a salary-paced transaction trajectory and document any large credits with payslips, Deposito maturities, or property-sale documentation.
  3. Indonesian-language document submission without certified translation. Marriage certificates (Buku Nikah / Akta Perkawinan), birth certificates (Akta Kelahiran), divorce decrees (Akta Cerai), and educational credentials (Ijazah / Transkrip) issued in Bahasa Indonesia require certified English translation plus often Kementerian Hukum dan HAM legalisation + Kemenlu (MOFA) attestation for Schengen and UK consulates. Documents submitted without proper certification chain are flagged "unverifiable."
  4. Mismatched flight or hotel bookings — particularly for Schengen. Indonesian applicants frequently submit non-refundable purchased tickets believing this strengthens the application. The opposite is true: visa officers treat verified PNR-coded reservations identically to purchased tickets for visa-decision purposes, and the financial loss on refusal is entirely avoidable. A single visa rejection on a IDR 25,000,000 Europe-bound ticket is a meaningful sum.
  5. Inconsistent travel narrative — first-time long-haul applicants. Schengen Form C-1 question 28 ("Purpose of journey") and question 21 ("Duration of stay") must be coherent with the applicant's profession, leave entitlement, and financial standing. A first-time international applicant claiming a 21-day Schengen multi-country tour without family hosts triggers immediate scrutiny — apply for shorter, clearly-purposed first trips (often Singapore/Thailand/Malaysia first, then build to longer destinations).

Indonesian applicants benefit measurably from a structured visa support letter that addresses each pattern explicitly: employment continuity with NPWP-traceable history, financial sourcing tied to documented earnings (with USD-equivalent context for currency-stable presentation), family ties via KTP/KK linkages, and a precise day-by-day plan in the format consular officers expect — particularly for Schengen Netherlands/Germany applications and UK family-visit cases.

Best Time to Apply — Seasonal Patterns

Visa application volumes from Indonesia follow predictable cycles tied to the Hajj/Umrah calendar, school holidays, the Eid (Idul Fitri) family-travel surge, and ASEAN seasonal patterns.

  • Avoid: April through August (peak Hajj season + Idul Fitri/Idul Adha + school summer holidays + ASEAN summer travel surge). Saudi processing prioritises Hajj-related visas, slowing other categories. Schengen consulates in Jakarta see 2–3× normal load; Italy and the Netherlands regularly extend their 15-day target to 25–30 days. UK Standard Visitor Visa wait times stretch from 3 weeks to 5–7 weeks.
  • Avoid: Idul Fitri week + week before Christmas/New Year. Indonesian government offices (Kemenkumham, Kemenlu, Imigrasi, Dukcapil for KTP/KK certifications) operate reduced or paused service. Plan supporting-document requests at least 2 weeks before any visa appointment in these windows.
  • Best: late January through mid-March. Post-Hajj quiet, Indonesian schools resumed, Umrah cycle has settled. Schengen and UK applications routinely process at the lower end of stated timelines. Indonesian Imigrasi e-passport upgrade processing is also fastest in this window.
  • Best: late September through October. A second efficient window between summer and end-of-year travel surges. Particularly good for Schengen, UK, US, and Australian applications planned for December–February travel.
  • Anchor your timeline. UK visitor: file at least 6 weeks before travel. Schengen: file 4–6 weeks before. US B1/B2: book the interview 2–6 months before intended travel (Indonesia waits have eased significantly since 2023). Australian visitor: 8–12 weeks. Saudi Hajj: timing is fixed by Ministry of Religious Affairs — ensure all documents are with your KBIH/PIHK 4–6 months before the Hajj season.
  • Idul Adha precedes Hajj. Indonesian Hajj travellers generally need to be cleared and at the airport 30–45 days before the actual Hajj — plan all family/employment-related supporting paperwork around this earlier-than-expected timeline.

Always file your application toward the start of the embassy's stated processing window — never the end. A 15-day Schengen visa applied for 14 days before travel will arrive late.

Currency & Money Tips

Indonesia's foreign-exchange framework, regulated by Bank Indonesia (BI) and Kementerian Keuangan, is more open than other large ASEAN markets but still requires careful documentation for visa applications.

  • Bank Indonesia outward forex policy. Individual outward USD/EUR purchases for travel are subject to bank-level limits (commonly USD 25,000–50,000 per trip for personal travel, with bank-discretion review on amounts above). Document every forex purchase via official receipts from licensed banks (Bank Mandiri, BCA, BRI, BNI, CIMB Niaga, Bank Maybank Indonesia, Bank Permata, Bank Danamon, BTN, HSBC Indonesia) — these are required at most embassy appointments.
  • Hajj / Umrah forex allocation is separate. Indonesian Hajj pilgrims operate under a fixed BPIH (Biaya Penyelenggaraan Ibadah Haji) framework that bundles forex with the package — verify with your KBIH/PIHK that the SAR allocation matches the current Hajj cap before departure.
  • Indonesian-issued debit/credit cards abroad. Most Indonesian-issued cards have monthly international transaction limits set by the issuing bank, commonly USD 1,500–5,000/month for premium tiers. Notify your bank before travel and request a temporary limit increase if needed.
  • Documenting "sufficient funds" for visa applications. Schengen consulates expect approximately EUR 60–80 per day for Indonesian applicants, evidenced by a 6-month bank statement plus a recent salary slip. UK visa officers typically look for GBP 80–100 per day for short visits, GBP 120 for family stays where the host provides accommodation. Australian visa officers look for AUD 5,000+ in liquid funds for visitor visas (more for student visas — verify current OSHC and accommodation thresholds).
  • NPWP (Tax ID) cross-reference. Schengen, UK, and Australian missions increasingly cross-reference NPWP details against income statements when assessing financial standing. An active, current NPWP is a baseline expectation — Indonesians without NPWP face higher scrutiny on income claims.
  • Deposito (Indonesian fixed-deposit certificates). A Deposito with at least 6 months of remaining tenure is treated by Schengen and UK officers as the strongest single proof of "ties to Indonesia." Keep a 12-month Deposito in a Bank Indonesia-licensed bank if you frequently apply for international visas.
  • Cash declaration thresholds. Indonesia requires declaration of inbound/outbound cash movements above IDR 100 million (approximately USD 6,500). Most destination countries have similar inbound declaration thresholds (Schengen €10,000, UK £10,000, US $10,000, Australia AUD 10,000) — declare anything close to or above.

Cultural Notes for Travelers

Indonesian travellers operate primarily in Bahasa Indonesia and English language environments, and consular officers across Schengen, UK, US, Canada, Australia, and the GCC are familiar with Indonesian document conventions — but several practical details still trip up first-time applicants.

  • Single-name passports. A meaningful share of Indonesian passports show only one name on the bio page (a long-standing Indonesian cultural pattern, particularly in Java). Schengen, UK, US, Australian, and Canadian visa application forms expect distinct "Given names" / "Surname" fields. The Schengen Visa Code recommends entering the single name in both the surname and given-name fields ("FNU" — First Name Unknown — was historically used in some US documents). Indonesian Imigrasi can issue an endorsement letter on the passport providing a "Surname-FNU" notation that resolves form-data mismatches at most consulates. Request this proactively if you hold a single-name passport.
  • Bahasa Indonesia documents need certified translation. Marriage certificates (Buku Nikah / Akta Perkawinan), divorce decrees (Akta Cerai), birth certificates (Akta Kelahiran), and educational certificates (Ijazah / Transkrip) issued in Bahasa Indonesia require certified English translation plus often a chain of legalisation: Notaris → Kementerian Hukum dan HAM → Kementerian Luar Negeri (Kemenlu/MOFA) for Schengen, UK, and many other consulates. Translations from a notary alone are sometimes rejected — use a Kemenlu-recognised sworn translator (Penerjemah Tersumpah).
  • e-passport adoption. Indonesia's e-passport rollout has dramatically improved acceptance at Schengen, UK, US, Australian, and Japanese missions — and is the prerequisite for Japan's bilateral visa-free arrangement. If you still hold a machine-readable passport (MRP), upgrade to e-passport before applying for any first-time long-haul visa or any Japan trip. Indonesian Imigrasi processes e-passport applications in 4–7 working days under the regular service in Jakarta and Surabaya.
  • KTP / Kartu Keluarga cross-references. Indonesian National ID (KTP) is a 16-digit number; the Family Card (Kartu Keluarga / KK) lists household members. Cross-reference these consistently across all visa application documents and supporting-document submissions — inconsistencies trigger automated flagging at major missions.
  • Photo specifications. Indonesian 3×4cm passport photos for Imigrasi differ from Schengen, UK, and US specifications (Schengen requires 35×45mm with 70–80% face coverage, neutral grey-white background). Always re-photograph at a VFS-affiliated provider for international visa appointments.
  • Dress code and gender-modesty considerations at biometric appointments. Schengen, UK, US, Canadian, and Australian biometric centres in Jakarta and Surabaya are formal environments. Business attire — including batik formal, hijab, and modest professional dress — is fully accepted. Women applying with hijab face no documentation issues for Schengen, UK, US, or Canadian visas; passport photos with hijab are accepted across all major missions.
  • Idul Fitri (Eid al-Fitr) and Idul Adha (Eid al-Adha). Indonesian government offices effectively pause for 7–10 days around Idul Fitri and 3–5 days around Idul Adha. Plan supporting-document requests (employment letters, KTP/KK certifications, MOFA attestations, NPWP statements) at least 3 weeks before any visa appointment in those windows.
  • English is sufficient for visa interviews. All major missions (UK, US, Canada, Schengen, Australia, GCC, Japan) conduct interviews in English. Bahasa Indonesia translators are not provided automatically — request in advance if needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many visa-free countries can Indonesian passport holders visit in 2026? +
In 2026, Indonesian passport holders can enter 45 countries completely visa-free, obtain visa-on-arrival in 31 additional countries, and apply for electronic visas (eVisa) to 34 more destinations. In total, 110 out of 198 tracked destinations (56%) are accessible without an advance embassy application.
Do Indonesian passport holders need a visa for Singapore? +

No. Indonesian passport holders are exempt from a visa for tourist or business stays in Singapore up to 30 days, under the ASEAN Framework Agreement on Visa Exemption. A confirmed return ticket and accommodation evidence may be requested at port of entry. Singapore remains the most-travelled destination for Indonesians for shopping, business, and short leisure trips. However, Indonesians intending to work in Singapore require an Employment Pass or appropriate work-visa category — visa-free entry covers tourism and business meetings only.

Is it true that Indonesians can enter Japan without a visa? +

Yes — but only with an e-passport. Japan grants visa-free entry to Indonesian passport holders carrying a valid biometric e-passport for tourist stays up to 15 days, under the bilateral arrangement introduced December 2014. Indonesians travelling on machine-readable passports (MRP) must apply for a tourist visa at the Embassy of Japan Jakarta or consulates in Surabaya, Medan, Denpasar, or Makassar. Action item: upgrade to e-passport at any major Indonesian Imigrasi office before planning Japan travel — processing is 4–7 working days.

How does the ASEAN visa-free arrangement work for Indonesians? +

Indonesia is a founding ASEAN member, and Indonesian passport holders enjoy visa-free entry to all nine fellow ASEAN states under the ASEAN Framework Agreement on Visa Exemption: Malaysia (30 days), Singapore (30 days), Thailand (30 days for tourism), Vietnam (30 days), Philippines (30 days), Cambodia (30 days), Laos (30 days), Myanmar (14 days, may require pre-registration), and Brunei (14 days). The visa-free arrangement covers tourism, family visits, and business meetings only — not employment, study, or long-term residence. Confirmed return ticket and accommodation evidence may be requested at port of entry.

Why does my Indonesian passport with only one name cause visa application problems? +

Many Indonesian passports show only a single name (a long-standing cultural convention, particularly in Java). Most international visa application forms expect distinct 'Given names' and 'Surname' fields. The Schengen Visa Code permits entering the single name in both fields, and Indonesian Imigrasi can issue an endorsement letter on the passport providing a 'Surname-FNU' (First Name Unknown) notation that resolves form-data mismatches at most consulates. Action item: if you have a single-name passport and frequently apply for international visas, request an Imigrasi endorsement letter proactively — it eliminates a common source of administrative refusal.

Can Indonesians get visa-on-arrival in the UAE? +

Yes. Indonesian passport holders qualify for free visa-on-arrival in the UAE for 30 days (extendable for an additional 30 days). No advance application needed. UAE is one of the most accessible major destinations for Indonesian travellers and serves as a major transit hub for Indonesian Hajj/Umrah pilgrims connecting to Saudi Arabia.

Why is the Schengen visa application process so detailed for Indonesians? +

Indonesian applicants face a multi-document Schengen process driven by historical refusal patterns and EU consular practice. Required documents typically include: passport (e-passport recommended), Schengen visa application form, recent photos meeting Schengen specs, confirmed flight reservation, hotel bookings covering the entire stay, travel insurance with €30,000 minimum medical coverage, 6-month bank statements, salary slip, employment letter (NPWP-registered employer), Indonesian tax certificate (NPWP), property/business documents, and Family Card (KK) cross-reference. The Netherlands is the dominant Schengen issuer to Indonesia (post-colonial bilateral ties); Germany, France, and Italy are also major issuers. VFS Global Jakarta and TLScontact handle most submissions.

How much is a Schengen visa for Indonesian applicants in 2026? +

The standard Schengen short-stay visa fee is €90 for adults and €45 for children aged 6–12, in effect since 11 June 2024 under EU Regulation 2023/2667. Additional VFS or TLScontact service charges apply (typically €25–€35) depending on which Schengen state's consulate you apply through. Netherlands, Germany, France, and Italy handle the largest share of Indonesian Schengen applications via VFS Global Jakarta and TLScontact.

What is the easiest country for Indonesian travellers to visit? +

Within ASEAN, all nine fellow members are visa-free for short stays — Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand are the most popular first international trips. Outside ASEAN: Maldives offers free 30-day visa-on-arrival, Hong Kong is visa-free for 30 days, Macao is visa-free, and Türkiye offers e-Visa applied online in minutes. Saudi Arabia tourist eVisa (since 2024) opens up direct tourism without going through Hajj/Umrah channels. Japan visa-free applies to e-passport holders for 15 days. These are typically the entry points for Indonesians building visa history.

What documents prove "ties to Indonesia" for a visa application? +

Strong ties demonstrate you will return after your trip. Most effective evidence: (1) Long-term employment with NPWP-registered employer and verifiable BPJS Kesehatan and Ketenagakerjaan deductions, (2) Property ownership (Sertifikat Hak Milik / SHGB / SHGB-strata title), (3) Active business registration with NIB under Sistem OSS, (4) Family dependents in Indonesia evidenced by KTP/Kartu Keluarga cross-references, (5) Recent Deposito (fixed-deposit) certificates from a Bank Indonesia-licensed bank with at least 6 months remaining tenure, (6) Children's school enrolment at an Indonesian institution. Pair these with current NPWP — Schengen, UK, Australian, and Canadian missions cross-reference NPWP details against income claims. The more layers, the stronger the case.
