Visa Types Explained: Visa-Free, Visa on Arrival, e-Visa, ETA & Embassy Visa

Before you book a trip, it helps to know which of the five visa categories applies to your passport and destination. Each one is obtained differently, costs a different amount and takes a different length of time. Use the checker above for your exact route, then read below to understand what your result means in 2026.

Visa-Free

Your passport lets you enter for short stays — usually 30 to 90 days — without applying for anything in advance. You simply arrive with a valid passport. Strong passports (Japan, Singapore, EU, UK) enjoy visa-free access to 150+ destinations. Border officers may still ask for an onward ticket and proof of funds.

Visa on Arrival (VOA)

You receive your visa at the airport or land border on arrival — no embassy visit beforehand. You typically pay a fee in cash or by card and present a passport photo and an onward ticket. Common in Thailand, Indonesia, Egypt, the Maldives and much of East Africa. Expect short queues at peak times.

e-Visa

You apply online before you travel and receive an electronic visa by email, usually within 24–72 hours. India, Australia, the UAE, Egypt, Kenya and Vietnam all run e-Visa portals. Most require a confirmed flight reservation — you can generate a free one with our dummy ticket tool instead of buying a non-refundable ticket.

Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA)

A lighter, pre-travel clearance linked to your passport — not a full visa. You answer a short online form and pay a small fee. Examples include the US ESTA, Canada eTA, and the EU ETIAS launching in 2026. Approval is often instant to 72 hours and is usually valid for multiple trips over 1–3 years.

Embassy / Consulate Visa

The traditional route: you apply in person (or via a visa centre such as VFS Global or BLS) at the destination country's embassy or consulate, submitting documents, biometrics and fees. Schengen, US, UK and Canada visas work this way, taking roughly 2–6 weeks. Required documents almost always include a flight reservation and proof of accommodation — both of which can be a free reservation, not a paid booking.

Key Visa Terms

Passport validity (six-month rule)
Many countries require your passport to stay valid for at least six months beyond your departure date. Renew early if you are close to expiry.
Onward / return ticket
Proof you intend to leave the country before your permission expires. A confirmed flight reservation is widely accepted — it does not have to be a paid ticket.
Multiple-entry visa
A visa that lets you enter a country several times within its validity, rather than just once.
Consular jurisdiction
The specific embassy or consulate responsible for your area of residence. Applying to the wrong one is a common cause of rejection.
Overstay
Remaining beyond your authorised stay. It can trigger fines, deportation and future entry bans, so always track your permitted days.
Schengen Area
29 European countries sharing one short-stay visa, allowing up to 90 days within any 180-day period across the whole zone.

Do I need a return ticket to enter a country?
+

Many countries require proof of onward travel — a return ticket or an onward flight booking to another destination — and airlines may check this before allowing you to board. If you don't have a return ticket yet, you can use our free dummy ticket generator to create a valid flight reservation as proof of onward travel.

What is the difference between visa-free and visa on arrival?
+

Visa-free means you can enter a country without any visa — just show your passport at immigration. Visa on arrival means you obtain a visa stamp or sticker at the airport when you arrive, which may involve filling out a form and paying a fee. Both allow entry without applying in advance, but visa on arrival may cost money and require additional documents.

What is an e-Visa?
+

An e-Visa (electronic visa) is a visa that you apply for and receive online, without visiting an embassy. You fill out an online application, upload documents, pay a fee, and receive your approval via email. Popular e-Visa destinations include India, Turkey, Australia (ETA), UAE, and Egypt. Processing times are usually faster than traditional visas (typically 24–72 hours).

How long can I stay in a visa-free country?
+

The allowed stay varies by country and your passport. Common visa-free durations include 14, 30, 60, or 90 days. Schengen Area countries allow 90 days within any 180-day period. Always check the specific rules for your passport-destination combination using the Visa Checker above.

What is the most powerful passport in the world?
+

As of 2026, the Japanese passport is consistently ranked as one of the most powerful in the world, offering visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to over 190 countries. Other top-ranking passports include Singapore, Germany, Spain, and South Korea. Use the Visa Checker above to see how many countries your passport gives you access to.

Can I work abroad with a tourist visa?
+

No. In most countries a tourist visa or visa-free entry does not permit employment. To work legally you typically need a work visa or work permit, which requires a job offer from an employer in that country. Working illegally can result in deportation, fines, and future visa denials. Some countries offer working holiday visas for young travelers.
