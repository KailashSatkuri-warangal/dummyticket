Peru Passport: Where Can You Travel in 2026?

The Peruvian passport ranks 39th–42nd globally in the 2026 Henley Passport Index, granting visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to roughly 135 destinations — among the strongest passports in Latin America after Chile, Argentina, and Brazil. With 34 million Peruvian citizens and an estimated 3.6 million Peruvians living abroad (concentrated in the United States, Spain, Argentina, Chile, Italy, and Japan), Peru combines a strong regional travel position with a historically underserved global advisory market.

The Peruvian visa-application landscape is shaped by three structural realities. First, Peru is included in Schengen Annex II (EU Regulation 2018/1806) — meaning Peruvians enter the Schengen area visa-free for tourist or business stays up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Once ETIAS becomes operational (late 2026 / early 2027), Peruvians will need to apply for ETIAS authorisation before each Schengen trip, but the visa-free privilege remains. Second, the Comunidad Andina (CAN) and Acuerdo de Residencia MERCOSUR frameworks allow Peruvians to travel and reside in Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador, Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Uruguay, and Paraguay with simplified documentation. Third, despite the strong passport, the United States is not on the visa-free list for Peru — Peruvian travellers must apply for a B1/B2 visa at the US Embassy Lima, where wait times for first-time interviews have been among the longest in Latin America in 2024–2026.

For frequent Peruvian travellers — students, young professionals, business operators, and the established diaspora visiting family — the central planning challenge in 2026 is twofold: navigating the upcoming ETIAS rollout for European travel, and managing the US B1/B2 wait times and renewal cadence. This guide details which destinations require advance authorisation, where Peru maintains diplomatic representation, and the specific supporting documents — Peruvian DNI cross-references, SUNAT tax records, RUC business documentation, and bank-issued forex receipts — that consular officers expect from Peruvian applicants in 2026.

  • United Kingdom — Visa-free for Peruvian passport holders for tourist stays up to 6 months. However, the UK introduced its own ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation) scheme in 2025 — Peruvians require an ETA at least 72 hours before travel; £10; valid 2 years or passport expiry.

Common Visa Rejection Reasons

Peruvian passport applicants encounter mid-tier rejection rates — better than most South Asian and African source countries thanks to the Schengen visa-free privilege, but with specific pattern weaknesses at US and Canadian missions where Peru still requires traditional visa processing.

  1. Insufficient evidence of return ties to Peru. The leading refusal cause for US B1/B2 and Canadian TRV applications. Strongest evidence: long-term employment with RUC-registered (Tax ID) employer and verifiable EsSalud and AFP/ONP deductions, property ownership (Inscripción Registral SUNARP), business registration with SUNAT (RUC active), dependents in Peru (DNI cross-references on RENIEC records), and recent fixed-deposit (Depósito a Plazo) certificates from a SBS-licensed bank.
  2. Bank statement issues — sudden deposits. US, Canadian, and (where applicable) UK ETA visa officers look closely at the 6-month transaction pattern in Peruvian bank statements. Sudden round-figure deposits (PEN 50,000 / 100,000 / USD 20,000) appearing 3–4 weeks before application are flagged as third-party funding. Maintain a salary-paced trajectory and document any large credits with payslips, Depósito maturities, or property-sale documents from SUNARP.
  3. US B1/B2 interview unpreparedness. Peruvian first-time B1/B2 applicants at the US Embassy Lima frequently underestimate the brevity and intensity of the consular interview. Vague answers about purpose of travel, weak ties to Peru, or inability to articulate trip funding sources typically result in 214(b) refusal — a non-immigrant visa refusal that is technically reversible but practically requires a stronger profile and re-application 3–6 months later.
  4. Schengen "implied permanent intent" issues for long visits. Even though Peru is visa-free for Schengen short stays, Peruvian travellers attempting longer-duration visits (close to 90 days) without strong return-ties documentation face increased border scrutiny — Spanish, Italian, and French border guards exercise discretion. Travellers planning back-to-back 90-day stays trigger automatic 90/180 calculation reviews.
  5. Document authentication gaps for non-Schengen destinations. Peruvian-issued civil records (Acta de Matrimonio, Acta de Nacimiento, Acta de Divorcio, Diploma educativo) increasingly require Apostille of The Hague legalisation for US, Canadian, Australian, and South Korean visa applications. Issued by Peru's Ministry of Foreign Affairs (Cancillería) and accepted across the 124+ Hague Convention member states. Documents submitted without Apostille (or with notarisation only) are flagged "unverifiable" — a near-automatic refusal.

Peruvian applicants benefit measurably from a structured visa support letter for US, Canadian, Australian, and Japanese applications that addresses each pattern explicitly: employment continuity with RUC-traceable history, financial sourcing tied to documented earnings, family ties via DNI/RENIEC records, and a precise day-by-day plan in the format consular officers expect.

Best Time to Apply — Seasonal Patterns

Visa application timing for Peruvian travellers is shaped by the US B1/B2 backlog at the Lima embassy, the Peruvian school calendar (March–December), the Andean carnival season, and summer travel surges to Spain and the US.

  • Avoid: December through March (Peruvian summer holidays + carnival + family-visit surge to US/Spain). US Embassy Lima B1/B2 wait times stretch significantly during these months; first-time interview slot availability tightens. Spanish and Italian Schengen border processing volumes rise.
  • Avoid: July (Peruvian Fiestas Patrias and mid-year vacation). Peruvian government offices (RENIEC for DNI, SUNARP for property, SUNAT for tax certifications, MRE for Apostille) operate reduced hours during the Fiestas Patrias week (28–29 July) and surrounding dates. Plan supporting-document requests at least 2 weeks before any visa appointment in late July.
  • Best: April through June. Post-summer-holiday quiet, schools in session, government offices fully staffed. US B1/B2 first-time interview slots typically more available; Apostille processing through MRE Lima at fastest cadence.
  • Best: September through November. A second efficient window between the Peruvian school year peak and the December–March surge. Particularly good for Schengen ETIAS applications (once operational), US B1/B2 applications, and Canadian TRV submissions for December–February travel.
  • Anchor your timeline. US B1/B2: book interview 6–18 months before intended travel during Lima peaks. Canada TRV: 8–12 weeks. Australian visitor: 2–4 months. Japan tourist visa: 2–3 weeks. Apostille of supporting documents through MRE Lima: 2–7 working days for the standard service.
  • ETIAS planning. Once operational (late 2026 / early 2027), apply at least 96 hours before any Schengen trip via the official ETIAS portal (travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en). Valid 3 years or passport expiry; €7. Do not assume same-day approval — the 96-hour minimum is a system rule.

Always file your visa or pre-authorisation application toward the start of the embassy's stated processing window — never the end. A US B1/B2 interview booked 6 weeks before travel during a Lima peak window will likely arrive too late.

Currency & Money Tips

Peru maintains one of the most stable currencies in Latin America and an open foreign-exchange framework — but visa applications still require structured financial documentation, particularly for US, Canadian, and Australian missions.

  • Banco Central de Reserva del Perú (BCRP) outward forex. Peru does not impose hard caps on outward USD/EUR purchases for individual travel — banks process forex requests up to documented travel needs. Bank-issued forex receipts remain mandatory for visa applications and re-entry checks. Use authorised banks: BCP, BBVA Perú, Interbank, Scotiabank Perú, Banco de la Nación, Banco de Crédito del Perú, Banco Falabella.
  • PEN ↔ USD stability. The Peruvian Sol has historically been one of the more stable Latin American currencies, which simplifies visa-application financial-statement presentation. US, Canadian, and Australian visa officers can read PEN balances directly without USD-equivalent conversion concerns (unlike post-devaluation EGP or post-2018 ARS contexts).
  • SUNAT (Peruvian Tax Authority) cross-references. US, Canadian, and Australian missions cross-reference RUC details against income statements when assessing financial standing. An active RUC (tax ID number) and current PDT (declaration jurada) filings are baseline expectations — Peruvians without active SUNAT records face higher scrutiny on income claims.
  • Documenting "sufficient funds" for visa applications. US B1/B2: the consular officer assesses funds during the interview rather than reviewing detailed bank statements. Bring a 6-month statement plus payslips and present them only if asked. Canadian TRV: detailed documentation required — typically CAD 2,000+ for short visits, more for student and longer-stay applications. Australian visitor: AUD 5,000+ in liquid funds for visitor visas. Once Schengen ETIAS is operational, the system will request approximate trip-cost evidence — 60–80 EUR/day is a reasonable Peruvian-applicant baseline.
  • Depósito a Plazo (Peruvian fixed-deposit certificates). A Depósito with at least 6 months remaining tenure is treated by US and Canadian visa officers as the strongest single proof of "ties to Peru." Keep a 12-month Depósito at a SBS-licensed bank if you frequently apply for international visas.
  • Cash declaration thresholds. Peru requires declaration of inbound/outbound cash above USD 30,000 (one of the higher thresholds in Latin America). Most destination countries have lower inbound declaration thresholds (Schengen €10,000, UK £10,000, US $10,000) — declare anything close to or above the destination's threshold.
  • AFP / ONP pension portability for diaspora. Peruvians living long-term in Spain, Italy, US, or Argentina under bilateral social-security agreements may transfer AFP/ONP pension contributions across systems. Verify current bilateral agreements with ONP before relocating.

Cultural Notes for Travelers

Peruvian travellers operate primarily in Spanish and English language environments, and consular officers across Schengen, US, Canadian, Australian, and Asian missions are familiar with Peruvian document conventions. A few practical points consistently catch Peruvian first-time applicants in 2026.

  • DNI vs Passport. The Peruvian DNI (Documento Nacional de Identidad) is the universal domestic ID and is sufficient for travel within the Andean Community (Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador) at land borders. For all other international travel (Schengen, US, Canada, Australia, Asia), a valid Peruvian passport is required. Peruvian passports are valid for 10 years for adults; child passports for 5 years. Renewal is processed through Migraciones offices in Lima, Arequipa, Trujillo, Piura, Cusco, and other regional centres.
  • Apostille of The Hague. Peru is a Hague Convention member since 2010 — Peruvian-issued civil records (Acta de Matrimonio, Acta de Nacimiento, Acta de Divorcio, Diploma educativo) require Apostille legalisation through the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores (MRE) Cancillería to be accepted at US, Canadian, Australian, South Korean, and most Asian visa applications. This single-step Apostille replaces the multi-step legalisation chains required of non-Hague countries — a meaningful simplification compared to Indonesian, Bangladeshi, or Pakistani documentation workflows.
  • Schengen visa-free privilege. Peru is on Schengen Annex II — Peruvian passport holders enter the Schengen area visa-free for short stays. This is a powerful privilege but introduces specific pitfalls: the 90/180 rolling-day calculation, the 3-month-validity-beyond-departure rule, and the 10-year-since-issue rule (passport must have been issued within the last 10 years on date of entry). Use the EU's official short-stay calculator at ec.europa.eu/assets/home/visa-calculator before any Schengen trip.
  • Names and titles. Peruvian passports follow the Spanish-language naming convention with two surnames (paternal and maternal). Embassy and visa application forms across the Anglosphere typically expect a single "Surname" field. Enter the paternal surname (the first surname on the Peruvian passport) in the surname field and both surnames in the "given names" or "middle names" field if required to capture the full name. Form-data mismatches are a common Schengen ETIAS application rejection cause.
  • DNI cross-references on RENIEC. Peruvian National ID is a 8-digit number issued by RENIEC. Cross-reference it consistently across all visa application documents — inconsistencies trigger automated flagging at major missions.
  • Photo specifications. Peruvian passport photos for Migraciones are 3×4cm with white background. Schengen, US, and Canadian visa application photos require 35×45mm with neutral grey-white background and 70–80% face coverage. Always re-photograph at a VFS-affiliated provider for international visa appointments.
  • Dress code at biometric appointments. US Embassy Lima, Canadian VFS Lima, and Australian visa centres are formal environments. Business attire is fully accepted; chuyo (traditional Andean hat) and other cultural attire are accepted but not standard for formal interview settings.
  • Fiestas Patrias (28–29 July). Peruvian government offices effectively pause for 7–10 days around Fiestas Patrias. Plan supporting-document requests (DNI certifications, SUNARP property records, MRE Apostille, SUNAT statements) at least 3 weeks before any visa appointment in late July.
  • English is sufficient for visa interviews. US, Canadian, Australian, UK, and most Asian missions conduct interviews in English. Spanish translators are available on request — confirm availability with the embassy in advance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many visa-free countries can Peruvian passport holders visit in 2026? +
In 2026, Peruvian passport holders can enter 90 countries completely visa-free, obtain visa-on-arrival in 37 additional countries, and apply for electronic visas (eVisa) to 33 more destinations. In total, 160 out of 198 tracked destinations (81%) are accessible without an advance embassy application.
Do Peruvian passport holders need a visa for Spain or other Schengen countries? +

No — for short tourist or business stays up to 90 days within any 180-day rolling period, Peruvian passport holders enter the Schengen area visa-free under EU Regulation 2018/1806 (Annex II). Once ETIAS becomes operational (expected late 2026 / early 2027), Peruvians will need to apply for ETIAS authorisation before each Schengen trip — €7, valid 3 years or until passport expiry. The 90/180 rolling-day rule still applies; track usage carefully using the EU's official short-stay calculator.

Is the United States visa-free for Peruvians? +

No. Peru is not on the US Visa Waiver Programme (VWP). Peruvian passport holders must apply for a B1/B2 non-immigrant visa at the US Embassy Lima before travel. Wait times for first-time interview slots have been among the longest in Latin America during 2024–2026, regularly exceeding 12 months. Renewals via the Interview Waiver (dropbox) programme process within 1–3 weeks if eligible (prior visa expired within 48 months, same category).

Why was China visa-free entry expanded for Peruvians? +

China expanded visa-free entry for Peruvian passport holders for tourist stays up to 30 days under a bilateral arrangement that took effect in June 2024 — replacing the prior visa-required regime. The expansion supports growing Peru–China trade ties, particularly around the inauguration of the Chancay megaport (a major Peru–China investment partnership). The 240-hour visa-free transit policy at major Chinese airports also applies for connection itineraries.

Can I travel to Bolivia, Colombia, and Ecuador with just my Peruvian DNI? +

Yes — for land border crossings within the Comunidad Andina (CAN), Peruvian DNI is sufficient for travel to Bolivia, Colombia, and Ecuador. No passport required at land borders. However, for air travel to these countries you typically need a passport. The CAN framework also simplifies residency applications across member states for longer stays.

Why does Apostille matter for my Peruvian visa application? +

Peru is a member of the Hague Convention since 2010. Peruvian-issued civil records (Acta de Matrimonio, Acta de Nacimiento, Acta de Divorcio, Diploma educativo) require Apostille of The Hague legalisation through the Ministerio de Relaciones Exteriores (Cancillería) to be accepted at US, Canadian, Australian, South Korean, and most Asian visa applications. This single-step Apostille replaces the multi-step legalisation chains required by non-Hague countries — a meaningful efficiency for Peruvian applicants. MRE Lima processes Apostille within 2–7 working days.

Do I need an ETA for the United Kingdom now? +

Yes. Since April 2025, the UK introduced an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) requirement for visa-exempt nationalities including Peruvian passport holders. Apply at gov.uk/guidance/apply-for-an-electronic-travel-authorisation-eta before travel — £10, valid 2 years or until passport expiry. The ETA allows visits up to 6 months. Apply at least 72 hours before travel (system requirement) to ensure approval in time for boarding.

Can Peruvians get visa-on-arrival in the UAE? +

Yes. Peruvian passport holders qualify for free visa-on-arrival in the UAE for 30 days. No advance application needed. UAE serves as a major transit hub for Peruvian travellers connecting to Asia, Africa, and the Indian Ocean.

What is the easiest country for Peruvian travellers to visit? +

Within Latin America: Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Bolivia, Ecuador, Mexico, Paraguay, Uruguay, Venezuela — all visa-free for short tourist stays. Mexico allows up to 180 days. Outside Latin America: Schengen Europe is visa-free (until ETIAS in late 2026); United Kingdom requires only a low-cost ETA; Türkiye is e-Visa applied online in minutes; Maldives offers free 30-day visa-on-arrival; China is visa-free for 30 days (bilateral expansion 2024). These are typically the entry points for Peruvians building visa history before applying for US, Canadian, or Australian visas.

What documents prove "ties to Peru" for a US or Canadian visa application? +

Strong ties demonstrate you will return after your trip. Most effective evidence: (1) Long-term employment with RUC-registered employer and verifiable EsSalud, AFP/ONP, and SUNAT-traceable payroll deductions, (2) Property ownership evidenced by Inscripción Registral SUNARP (registry inscription), (3) Active business registration with SUNAT (current RUC, PDT filings up to date), (4) Family dependents in Peru evidenced by DNI cross-references on RENIEC, (5) Recent Depósito a Plazo (fixed-deposit) certificates from a SBS-licensed bank with at least 6 months remaining tenure, (6) Children's school enrolment at a Peruvian institution. The more layers, the stronger the case at US Embassy Lima B1/B2 interviews and Canadian TRV applications.
